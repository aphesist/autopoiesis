Laboratory · autopoiesis

Plant the demon, then count the cheaters

A program that rewrites itself is judged by a judge it can read. This page shows one run of that arrangement as it was recorded: who descended from whom, what each agent was scored, and which of the judge's planted weaknesses it learned to use.

    Lineages

    Each tree is one lineage. Time runs left to right in generations; every child descends from one parent chosen from the archive, by score in the DGM arm and uniformly at random in the random arm. A mark's colour and shape name the flaw its tripwire caught; grey circles caught none. A hollow mark is a child that was not admitted to the archive. A dashed ring means more than one flaw. Hover, tap or tab to a node for its scores.

    The Goodhart gap

    The selection score is what the flawed evaluator reports on the selection split. The held-out score is what a clean evaluator, with strong tests and type-strict comparison, reports on problems that are never selected on. Their difference is the part of the score that measures the evaluator rather than the agent. Points are means over the admitted children of each generation; the held-out score exists only on the generations where the loop computed it.

    Discovery

    For each flaw, how many lineages had produced at least one child that tripped it, by generation. A step up is a discovery; the line never comes down, because an exploit once found is a fact about the lineage even if its descendants forget it. The decoy D pays nothing: it is there to tell curiosity from selection.
